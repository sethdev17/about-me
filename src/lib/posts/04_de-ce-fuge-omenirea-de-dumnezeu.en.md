We live in an era of profound <span class="term-tooltip">paradoxes<span class="info-icon">i</span><span class="tooltip-bubble">Situations or statements that seem contradictory, yet express a hidden truth.</span></span>. If you look carefully at today's society, you will notice a bizarre and tragic tendency: people often promote darkness, vice and self-destruction, glorifying a chaotic lifestyle, while the idea of God, of good and of hope is met with scepticism, mockery or even <span class="term-tooltip">hostility<span class="info-icon">i</span><span class="tooltip-bubble">A hostile attitude, full of hatred.</span></span>. Meanwhile, anyone who dares to speak of God, of balance, light and hope is regarded with reluctance, considered old-fashioned, judged or even pushed to the margins. Why is it so easy for man to embrace evil and so hard to promote good? The answer to this question hides our deepest fear: <span style="text-decoration: 1px underline;">the fear of taking responsibility for the meaning of our own existence.</span>

**1. The mirage of "absolute freedom" and the trap of debauchery / The fear of responsibility and the dictatorship of one's own weaknesses**

<p class="fara-alineat">The main reason people flee from the Divine is the illusion of limitation. The idea of “God” implies an uncomfortable truth: that there is an objective morality and that our actions have consequences. Darkness, on the other hand, asks for nothing. It offers an illusion of absolute freedom, a space in which man believes himself to be his own master.</p>

<p class="fara-alineat">But what does this <span class="term-tooltip">debauched<span class="info-icon">i</span><span class="tooltip-bubble">To give oneself over to dissipation, to lead an unrestrained life of easy pleasures.</span></span> freedom really mean? Living from club to club, taking refuge in alcohol and drugs, burning yourself out in <span class="term-tooltip">fleeting<span class="info-icon">i</span><span class="tooltip-bubble">Short-lived / passing relationships.</span></span>, superficial relationships – none of this is authentic freedom, but the cruellest form of slavery. When you are unable to say “no” to your own impulses, you are no longer free; you are dictated to by your vices. People chase instant <span class="term-tooltip">gratification<span class="info-icon">i</span><span class="tooltip-bubble">To grant something to someone. / To give someone a favour, a reward.</span></span>, looking for quick doses of dopamine to cover an inner void. Yet when the deafening noise of the parties dies down, man is left alone before his own unfulfillment. A freedom without morality is merely a squandering of life on <span class="term-tooltip">ephemeral<span class="info-icon">i</span><span class="tooltip-bubble">Something passing / temporary / short-lived.</span></span> things, which leave you just as empty inside before you return to the earth.</p>

**2. Religion vs. a personal relationship with God**

<p class="fara-alineat">Much of this rejection also comes from a fundamental confusion between religion and spirituality. As mortal beings, we do not possess absolute truth, and religious <span class="term-tooltip">dogmas<span class="info-icon">i</span><span class=tooltip-bubble>Fundamental teachings of a religion, which cannot be subjected to criticism.</span></span>, throughout history, have often been altered or used by people for control and indoctrination.</p>

<p class="fara-alineat">But seeking God does not mean converting blindly to a doctrine. You do not have to follow human rules mechanically; you have to use your reason and your soul to build an intimate, personal relationship with that supreme force you truly feel to be real. To study, to analyse what is logical, and to connect with the Divine without intermediaries. It is not about indoctrination, but about opening your mind to the idea that we are not merely a biological accident in a cold universe.</p>

**3. Faith as a pillar of safety in a fallen world**

<p class="fara-alineat">The reality is that the world can be a harsh, unfair and exhausting place, and life's hardships are simply inevitable. Depression, despair, betrayals and, ultimately, death are realities no one escapes. Yes, some people may proudly claim they can overcome anything on their own, but most break down without a pillar to lean on, often realising too late that society is negative, that people can easily disappoint you and, in the end, forget you. In this context of uncertainty, why would we refuse a source of hope? This is where the profound <span class="term-tooltip">pragmatism<span class="info-icon">i</span><span class="tooltip-bubble">A philosophical tradition centred on the question of the meaning of truth.</span></span> of faith comes in. Even if you question the absolute reality of the Divine, logically speaking you have absolutely nothing to lose by choosing to believe, and only a life to gain. A personal relationship with God gives you a pillar of stability that no one can tear down, your own anchor of safety that makes you stronger, more <span class="term-tooltip">resilient<span class="info-icon">i</span><span class="tooltip-bubble">Resistant</span></span> in the face of suffering, more alive and happier.</p>

<p class="fara-alineat">If God did not exist, by choosing to believe you would still have lived a life full of meaning, balance and peace, avoiding the trap of <span class="term-tooltip">nihilism<span class="info-icon">i</span><span class="tooltip-bubble">A way of thinking specific to any philosophical doctrine that sets out to radically deny a system of values.</span></span> and that deep sadness born of the fear that you existed in vain. And if He does exist, you have won eternity. <span class="term-tooltip"><a href="https://ro.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pariul_lui_Pascal" style="color: red; inherit;">Observation<span class="info-icon">i</span><span class="tooltip-bubble">Referring to Pascal's Wager: a philosophical argument stating that it is more rational to believe in God than not to.</span></span> Unfortunately, it is precisely this inner peace that bothers people. Many, being unhappy and directionless themselves, will try to drag you down and attack your faith. Their mockery is not an argument against the Divine, but a defensive reaction: they do it precisely because your light and hope expose their own darkness.</p>

**4. The great stumbling block: the death of the innocent, suffering and betrayed expectations**

<p class="fara-alineat">Here, inevitably, comes the hardest argument of those who reject faith: <span style="font-style: italic;">Why should I believe in a Divinity that disappoints me? How can I believe when I see babies, innocent children or unborn infants dying for no reason? If God exists and loves us, why does He allow so much suffering and not save them? And if they were not meant to live, why were they born at all, only to suffer?</span> <br>It is a heartbreaking question, one that wears away the conscience of any rational person.</p>

<p class="fara-alineat">Yet the fundamental error in this revolt is that we see God as the direct author of evil, as a tyrant who invents suffering to test us. The reality is that we live in a fragile biological world, governed by natural laws and by the consequences of human actions. The Divine does not send illness or tragedy as an instrument of educational torture; they are the result of this imperfect world. What God does is not cause suffering, but redeem it. When tragedy strikes, a higher force does not waste that pain; it turns it into a <span class="term-tooltip">catalyst<span class="info-icon">i</span><span class="tooltip-bubble">A fact that hastens the unfolding of an action or an event.</span></span> that can crush pride, awaken unconditional love or remind us how precious life is. We, with our limited minds, judge life like a timed transaction, forgetting that we see only the tangled underside of a cosmic <span class="term-tooltip">tapestry<span class="info-icon">i</span><span class="tooltip-bubble">A decorative fabric of wool or silk, worked by hand.</span></span>.</p>

<p class="fara-alineat">From a spiritual perspective, the death of a pure soul that has not yet sinned is an immense tragedy for us who remain here, but for that soul it means only a swift passage through the gate of existence, receiving the gift of eternity without having to endure the corruption of this world. To be angry with the Divine for not suspending the laws of nature in order to give you the ending you expected means, in fact, wishing that you were your own God.</p>

<p class="fara-alineat"><span style="font-weight: bold;">Silence</span> or <span style="font-weight: bold;">Failure</span> are often <span class="term-tooltip">catalysts<span class="info-icon">i</span><span class="tooltip-bubble">A fact that hastens the unfolding of an action or an event.</span></span> for growth. Let's be realistic: if the Divine intervened to save us every time we hit a rough patch, what kind of beings would we become? We would be like spoiled children, incapable of developing empathy, courage, resilience or wisdom. Human virtues are not forged in comfort, but in pain. An answer of “No”, or absolute silence in the face of a desperate plea, does not necessarily mean that God is absent. Sometimes the greatest act of love of a higher force is to let you go through the storm, because only that way can you discover how strong you are, and only that way can you mature spiritually.</p>

<p class="fara-alineat">People often use these dramas to give up on the idea of God altogether, angry at Him. But here is the <span class="term-tooltip">paradox<span class="info-icon">i</span><span class="tooltip-bubble">Situations or statements that seem contradictory, yet express a hidden truth.</span></span>: a real relationship also involves crises. If you revolt with such fury against suffering, you do so precisely because deep inside you feel there must be an absolute standard of Justice and Good. If we were merely a biological accident, death would be a simple banality of nature, not a heartbreaking tragedy. The fact that you are angry with God, that you demand an account from Him and feel disappointed in Him shows, in fact, that your soul is looking for answers and refuses to accept chaos. You cannot quarrel with a reality you are absolutely convinced does not exist. The opposite of faith and love is not fury, but cold indifference. Your fury shows that somewhere deep within you there is a claim, and a hidden hope, that someone should have intervened.</p>

<p class="fara-alineat" style="font-weight: bold;">The conclusion?</p>

<p class="fara-alineat">At the end of our journey on this earth, we all face the same certainty: <span style="color: #ff0000; font-weight: bold; ">death</span>. Those who have lived only for fleeting pleasures, defying any morality, risk leaving this world with an overwhelming sense of futility, realising that they chased after trifles. To place your hope in a Divinity and choose to be a good person is not proof of weakness, but of immense courage. It is the only way we can rise above an apparently endless cycle of life and death, giving real meaning to our existence. True fulfilment of the soul is not found in the chaos of the world, but in that inner anchor of peace and hope that only a personal relationship with the Divine can offer.</p>
