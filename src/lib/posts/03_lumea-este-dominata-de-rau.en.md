We live in a reality in which, more often than not, good seems to be the exception, and evil an unwritten rule of survival. If we look honestly around us, we notice that we do not need supernatural forces to explain suffering; it springs from a far more earthly source: a chronic lack of empathy. We have grown used to walking past those who need help, to looking away and letting ourselves be swallowed by an indifference that has gradually become our new armour.

<p class="fara-alineat">This cruelty shows itself not only in great deeds, but also in small, <span class="term-tooltip">everyday<span class="info-icon">i</span><span class="tooltip-bubble">Familiar</span></span> gestures. We have reached a point where most people tend to judge, mock or humiliate anyone who dares to be different. Instead of offering a compliment or affirming the beauty in the other, we often choose the “weapon” of derision to feel, temporarily, superior. It is a form of social poison that seems to run through our veins, an inclination to do harm not because we are possessed, but simply because humanity has failed to cultivate its own kindness.</p>

<p class="fara-alineat">What is more, evil has become refined. If physical violence is still present and painful, psychological violence has become omnipresent. It is subtler, harder to prove and to punish, but it leaves deep marks on the mental structure of its victims. This inhumanity creeps in everywhere: into the institutions that should protect us, where even those invested with authority sometimes become sources of terror, and also into the most sacred place of all – the family.</p>

<p class="fara-alineat">In our homes, where there should be peace, we often find quarrels, lies and abuse. There is a cruel inability to find alternatives to an education based on fear, or to solving conflicts through words that cut deeper than a knife. Many of us are reduced to primal reactions, lacking the emotional resources needed to understand that the traumas created today will become tomorrow's monsters for our children.</p>

<p class="fara-alineat">Looking to the peaks of power, we see the same image mirrored on a large scale. Governments, those who hold the reins of the world, are often dominated by the same moral squalor as the ordinary man, but with an infinitely more destructive impact. In this configuration, it is hard to keep hoping for a “superior race”. As long as 90% of the world is dominated by these dark impulses, our progress as a species remains stuck in a delayed spiritual adolescence.</p>

<p class="fara-alineat">Real evolution will not come from technology, but from an awakening of consciousness. Until we have leaders capable of governing without corruption, and until ordinary people choose empathy over indifference, we will keep writing the same chapters of suffering. Evil has existed for centuries, and its greatest victory is that it has convinced us it is inevitable. But perhaps our only chance is that 10% of good that still flickers, waiting for humanity to finally wake from this sleep of reason.</p>
