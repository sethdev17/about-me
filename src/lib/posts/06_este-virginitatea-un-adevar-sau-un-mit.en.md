Today I want to bring up a subject that continues to haunt modern society, especially among young people: **the myth of virginity and the label of “purity”**.

<p class="fara-alineat">We are still being bombarded with the archaic idea that a person “loses” something essential with their first sexual experience, automatically becoming less valuable or “less pure”. These stereotypes are nothing but constructs born of fear, lack of education and some people's desire to put labels on others in order to make them feel inferior.</p>

<p class="fara-alineat">The reality, however uncomfortable it may be for some, is a simple one: <span style="font-weight: bold;">from a strictly biological point of view, virginity does NOT exist.</span></p>

---

### 1. The biological illusion: What does science actually say?

Virginity is not a medical term. It is a social and religious concept that we have forcibly tried to attach to anatomy. 

<p class="fara-alineat">Women are the ones most affected by this judgement, based on the greatest anatomical myth of all: <span style="font-weight: bold;">the hymen</span>.</p>

* Many people imagine the hymen as an airtight “seal” that is dramatically broken on first contact. In reality, the hymen is just a thin, elastic fold of mucosa that lines the vaginal opening.
* It is **already naturally perforated** from childhood – otherwise the female body would not be able to shed menstruation.
* The hymen stretches, adapts and can gradually thin over the years through movement, sport or the use of internal tampons. 
* And the bleeding on the first occasion is not an “obligatory proof of purity”. In most cases, pain and bleeding occur because of fear, lack of lubrication and the involuntary contraction of muscles, fuelled precisely by these absurd myths.

<p class="fara-alineat">In men, the biological illusion is even more obvious: there is absolutely no physical change, no tissue that tears or alters after the first sexual encounter. If the frenulum (the string) tears, that is an accident caused by a particular anatomy (short frenulum), by no means a rule of “initiation”.</p>

---

### 2. Virginity as a purely psychological block

If, from a medical point of view, you lose nothing and change nothing physically, why is this notion so powerful?

<p class="fara-alineat">Because virginity exists <span style="font-weight: bold;">only on a psychological level</span>. It is that mental barrier linked to lack of experience: you don't know how it feels, you have never been through it, you are nervous and vulnerable. When the act is consummated, no part of you is “lost”; you have simply gained a new life experience. You are exactly the same person before and after.</p>

<p class="fara-alineat">And yet society uses this psychological threshold to create a toxic hierarchy:</p>

* If you do it “too early”, you are judged and considered shallow.
* If you haven't done it by a certain age, you are ridiculed and pointed at (“look, the virgin”).

<p class="fara-alineat">It is a huge hypocrisy. We obsessively look for “pure”, inexperienced partners, forgetting that experience, communication and emotional maturity are exactly what make an intimate relationship harmonious and healthy.</p>

---

### 3. Your body, your decision

<p class="fara-alineat">Each of us has our own rhythm, our own feelings and our own choices. No one has the right to decide how many people you should be intimate with, when you should start or whether you should wait. As long as your actions are based on consent and harm no one, your private life concerns strictly two people.</p>

<p class="fara-alineat">If you want to be sure about your health and your partner's, <span style="font-weight: bold;">you don't look for the myth of virginity</span>; you get a routine medical test for sexually transmitted infections. That is responsibility and maturity, not the archaic verification of someone's past.</p>

---

### In conclusion...

<p class="fara-alineat">Virginity is an idea invented by rigid minds that wanted to measure human dignity through a moralistic filter.</p>

<p class="fara-alineat">The moment you get rid of this complex in your mind and realise that your worth as a person does not lie in the number of experiences you have had (be it zero or a hundred), you gain an immense inner freedom. Don't let stereotypes dictate your self-esteem. You are a complete person regardless of your intimate past.</p>
